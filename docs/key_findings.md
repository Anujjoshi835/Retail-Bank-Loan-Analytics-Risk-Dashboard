# Key Findings

Overall: 255,347 loans, total loan amount about 32.58 billion, average income 82,499, average credit score 574, 29,653 defaults (11.61 percent).

## Default rate by segment (percent)

| Segment | Highest | Lowest |
|---|---|---|
| Age group | 18 to 29: 19.71 | 60 and over: 5.05 |
| Income band | Under 50k: 17.16 | Over 100k: 9.10 |
| Employment | Unemployed: 13.55 | Full-time: 9.46 |
| Education | High School: 12.88 | PhD: 10.59 |
| Credit score band | Under 600: 12.46 | Over 700: 10.21 |
| Marital status | Divorced: 12.53 | Married: 10.40 |
| Loan purpose | Business: 12.33 | Home: 10.23 |
| Co-signer | No co-signer: 12.87 | With co-signer: 10.36 |
| Mortgage | No mortgage: 12.35 | With mortgage: 10.88 |

## Reading the results

- Age and income are the strongest separators. Borrowers under 30 default at nearly four times the rate of those over 60, and borrowers earning under 50k at nearly twice the rate of those above 100k.
- A co-signer and a mortgage each go with a lower default rate of about 1.5 to 2.5 points.
- Credit score has a smaller effect than expected in this data: the gap between the poor and excellent bands is about 2 points.
- The book itself is evenly spread: every purpose, education level, employment type and marital status holds close to an equal share of loans, and average loan amount is about 127k whatever the purpose or term.
